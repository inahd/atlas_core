## 1. Identity

### 1.1 Substance

Zinc is the chemical element with symbol Zn and atomic number 30. Among the dhatus, zinc has distinctive properties:

- **Biological essentiality**: zinc is required by every cell; ~300 enzymes use zinc as cofactor; immune function, wound healing, taste, smell, reproduction all require zinc
- **Non-cumulative toxicity at therapeutic doses**: unlike lead, mercury, or copper, zinc has wide safety margin (RDA 8-11 mg adult; toxicity threshold ~150-200 mg/day for chronic exposure)
- **Reactivity**: oxidizes readily in moist air; reacts with most acids; not as inert as gold/silver, but more stable than iron
- **Hexagonal close-packed (HCP) crystal structure**: makes zinc unique among dhatus (most others are FCC or BCC)
- **Distinctive relationship to ZnO**: zinc oxide has wurtzite phase that is naturally bioactive — antimicrobial, UV-absorbing, semiconductor properties

Physical properties:
- Density: 7.14 g/cm³ at 20°C
- Melting point: 419.53°C (similar to tin and lead in being relatively low)
- Boiling point: 907°C
- Color: bluish-silver metallic when freshly cut, oxidizing to dull grey
- Hardness: 2.5 Mohs
- HCP crystal structure (lattice constants a=2.66, c=4.95 Å)

### 1.2 Nomenclature

Per *Rasaratna Samuccaya* and the *Charaka Samhita*, zinc's Sanskrit names include:

- **Yashada** (यशद) — most common technical term; "fame-giving"
- **Jasada** (जसद) — alternative spelling, common in some regional traditions
- **Yashomandala** (यशोमण्डल) — "circle of fame"
- **Reetimandala** (रीतिमण्डल) — alternative term
- **Sweta-loha** (श्वेत-लोह) — "white metal" reference
- **Khurada** (खुरद) — "hoof-shaped" or "hard-edged" reference

The terminology emphasizes positive associations (fame, brightness) consistent with zinc's good reputation in classical practice as a beneficial substance.

### 1.3 Graha correspondence

Zinc corresponds to **Guru** (Jupiter) in the classical Indian astrological framework. *This is the same correspondence as tin* (Volume VI). The unanimous attribution of zinc to Guru across primary sources:
- *Brihat Parashara Hora Shastra* (BPHS) Chapter 3
- *Brihat Samhita* (Varahamihira) on planetary metallurgy
- *Rasaratna Samuccaya* graha-rasa identifications
- *Charaka Samhita* on therapeutic correspondences

**The shared Guru correspondence with tin has structural significance:**

Both zinc and tin are:
- Soft and malleable (lower hardness than iron, copper)
- Lower melting points (zinc 420°C, tin 232°C — both well below puta temperatures)
- Beneficial in alloys (zinc creates brass with copper; tin creates bronze)
- Associated with abundance and improved-properties (Jupiter-quality)
- Bio-compatible (zinc is essential nutrient; tin in food cans is safe)

The grouping reflects Jupiter-quality observable properties: substances that *enhance* and *expand* (alloy partner, beneficial nutrient, growth-supporting) rather than substances that *constrain* and *structure* (Saturn-quality of iron and lead).

Within the dhatu/jeeva/moola classification:
- **Guru is jeeva-graha** — the "living/cognitive" class (same as Surya-gold and Budha-mercury)

Friendship pattern of Guru in BPHS naisargika (cross-reference Volume VI §1.4):
- Guru is friend of: Surya (Sun), Chandra (Moon), Mangala (Mars)
- Guru is enemy of: Budha (Mercury), Shukra (Venus)
- Guru is neutral to: Shani (Saturn), Rahu, Ketu

**Same Guru pattern as tin** — friendship with Surya, Chandra, Mangala; enmity with Budha and Shukra. Same predictions about favorable nakshatras apply to zinc operations as to tin operations.

### 1.4 Origins and Sources

Classical Indian zinc sources:
- **Jawar mines (Rajasthan)** — major historical zinc-lead deposits; world's earliest large-scale zinc smelting (~12th-13th c. CE archaeological evidence at Zawar)
- **Aravalli ranges** — multiple zinc-lead deposits across the region
- **South-East Asia trade** — zinc from Burma and other regions historically imported
- **Smaller deposits** — distributed across multiple regions

India was historically a major zinc producer. The *Zawar* zinc smelting tradition (~13th-19th c.) used a unique downward-distillation furnace technology that pre-dates similar European methods by several centuries. Indian zinc metallurgy reached significant technological sophistication in the medieval period.

Notable historical fact: **Zinc was distinguished as a separate metal in Indian metallurgy long before it was identified in Europe.** European chemistry recognized zinc as a distinct element only in the 18th century (Andreas Marggraf, 1746); Indian metallurgy had been working with pure zinc as a separate substance from at least the medieval period, possibly earlier.

## 2. The Shodhana–Marana Sequence

### 2.1 Shodhana (Purification)

Per *Rasaratna Samuccaya* and modern characterization studies, zinc purification follows a modified version of the standard sequence:

**Procedure:**
1. **Taila (sesame oil)** — heat zinc to ~350-400°C (well below 420°C melting point), quench 7 times
2. **Takra (buttermilk)** — quench 7 times
3. **Go-mutra (cow's urine)** — quench 7 times
4. **Aranala (fermented kanji)** — quench 7 times
5. **Kulattha-kvatha (horse-gram decoction)** — quench 7 times

For zinc specifically, additional vishesha shodhana steps may use *Churnodaka* (lime-water) or specific herbal media depending on the regional tradition.

**Modern chemical interpretation:**

Each of the five quenching media interacts with zinc's surface differently. Zinc's higher reactivity than gold/silver means each step does substantial chemical work:
- **Sesame oil**: organic medium provides reducing atmosphere; sulfur compounds in oil produce thin protective layer
- **Buttermilk**: lactic acid removes ZnO surface layer
- **Cow urine**: alkaline-ammoniacal environment; zinc forms soluble zinc-ammine complexes
- **Kanji**: acidic, removes residual oxide
- **Kulattha**: amino-acid mediated surface conditioning

The cycling between oxidizing and reducing media is effective for zinc because zinc readily transitions between metallic Zn⁰ and ionic Zn²⁺. The cycling actively works the zinc surface, removing iron, lead, and copper contaminants common in raw zinc.

**Modern characterization of intermediates** (NOPR/NIScPR 2021): The Samanya and Vishesha Shodhit Yashad shows distinct XRD pattern from raw zinc, indicating chemical-physical changes have begun at the shodhana stage.

**Reproducibility:** High. Standard procedure across licensed Ayurvedic pharmacies.

### 2.2 Jarana (Heating with Plant Extracts)

**Zinc-specific step**: between shodhana and marana, zinc undergoes *jarana* with plant extracts (typically including *Azadirachta indica* / neem leaves and *Aloe vera* gel).

**Procedure:**
1. Purified zinc is melted in an open pan (zinc melts at 420°C)
2. Plant extracts are added in specific proportions
3. The mixture is stirred and heated at controlled temperature
4. The product, *Jarit Yashad*, is a fine powder

**Modern chemical interpretation:**

This step partially converts zinc to ZnO and provides organic surface coating:
- Zn (metal) + O₂ + organic matrix → ZnO + organic surface compounds
- The plant extracts provide:
  - Reducing agents (organic compounds prevent excessive oxidation early)
  - Surface-active compounds (antimicrobial neem alkaloids, aloe polysaccharides)
  - Possibly chelating agents (binding contaminant metals)

XRD analysis of Jarit Yashad shows partial ZnO formation alongside residual metallic zinc. This is intermediate stage chemistry.

### 2.3 Marana (Incineration)

The transformation from purified zinc to *yashada-bhasma* through repeated puta cycles. *This is where the empirical data is richest.*

**Procedure (per ACS Omega 2023, multiple PMC papers):**
1. *Shuddha Yashad* (purified, jarit zinc) is rolled into thin pieces
2. Triturated with herbal extracts (typically *Azadirachta indica* leaves and *Aloe vera* gel)
3. Cakes formed and dried
4. Sealed in *sharava-samputa*
5. Subjected to *puta* heating cycles
6. Modern preparations may use electric muffle furnace at 600-700°C
7. **17 puta cycles** for full preparation in canonical preparations

**Modern chemical interpretation:**

Zinc oxidation under puta conditions is straightforward:
- Zn (metallic, +0) + ½O₂ → ZnO (zinc oxide, +2)
- ZnO is thermodynamically stable; white-grey colored as bulk; transparent when nanoscale; semiconductor properties; UV-absorbing
- Crystal structure: hexagonal wurtzite (rare among bhasmas — most others are tetragonal or cubic)

The classical procedure achieves complete conversion to ZnO through repeated heating cycles. Modern characterization documents progressive crystal-size reduction:

**ACS Omega 2023 study** (Hitachi FESEM, HRTEM, XRD across 17 puta cycles):
- **Cycle 1-5**: rod-shaped 1.5 μm zinc particles + partial ZnO formation
- **Cycle 6-12**: progressive transition to ZnO; particle size reduces; crystallite size reduces from 53.14 to 42.40 nm
- **Cycle 13-17**: 31 nm particle morphology; some 10 nm nanoparticles confirmed by HRTEM
- **Crystal phase**: hexagonal ZnO wurtzite throughout, with progressive lattice strain reduction

**Modern characterization (peer-reviewed):**

| Reference | Method | Findings |
|-----------|--------|----------|
| Bhowmick et al. 2018 (PMC7527797) | XRD, DLS, SEM, FTIR, EDAX, zeta potential | Hexagonal ZnO wurtzite; crystallite size 32.79 nm; DLS 339.8 nm; SEM 324 nm; high zeta potential confirms stability |
| ACS Omega 2023 (Sant et al.) | XRD, FESEM, HRTEM, FTIR, Raman, SAXS | 17-puta complete characterization; 53→42 nm crystallite reduction; 10 nm nano-features in finished product |
| NOPR/NIScPR 2021 | XRD throughout shodhana-jarana-marana | Phase identification at each stage; ZnO confirmation at end |
| Santhosh et al. 2013 | XRD, parikshas | Zinc carbonate intermediate phase identified; ZnO end-product |
| Multiple antimicrobial studies | MIC, IC50, cell viability | IC50 >1000 μg/mL Caco-2 cells (essentially non-toxic); antimicrobial activity confirmed against gram-pos and gram-neg bacteria |

**Convergent finding**: traditional yashada-bhasma is **hexagonal ZnO (wurtzite phase) at the nanoscale**, with crystallite size 30-50 nm and particle agglomerates 320-340 nm. The chemistry is consistent across multiple independent studies. *The particle size data is unusually rich and quantitatively detailed for any bhasma.*

**Variation by mediation route**:
- **Standard procedure** (herbal mediation): ZnO wurtzite primary
- **Parada Marita** (mercury-mediated): **ZnS sphalerite primary** with minor ZnO
- **Hartala Marita** (orpiment-mediated): ZnO primary with minor ZnS

This means three distinct products from the same starting metal depending on mediation chemistry. The classical texts allow all three routes; modern licensed pharmacy varies.

**Reproducibility:** High. Multiple independent studies confirm consistent ZnO phase identity. The variation between traditional and modern muffle-furnace methods is characterized; particle size and crystallite reduction are quantified.

### 2.4 The traditional confirmation tests

The standard tests apply: *rekhapurnatva*, *varitaratva*, *apunarbhava*, *niruttha*, *nirdhuma*, *niswadu*, *nischandratva*. For zinc specifically:
- *Rekhapurnatva* particularly important — confirms particle size <10 μm; modern characterization shows particles ≤340 nm typical
- *Apunarbhava* — confirms ZnO is stable and won't revert to elemental zinc

These tests work well for zinc-bhasma. The classical QC system was empirically calibrated to detect proper ZnO formation, which corresponds directly to modern XRD validation.

## 3. Major Zinc Preparations

### 3.1 Yashada-Bhasma (Zinc Oxide Nanoparticles)

**Composition:** ZnO (zincite/wurtzite phase) primarily; minor herbal organic content; specific mediation routes can produce ZnS (sphalerite) as primary phase  
**Color:** White to pale yellow  
**Particle size:** Crystallite ~30-50 nm; particle agglomerates ~320-340 nm  
**Source text:** Rasaratna Samuccaya, Bhaishajya Ratnavali, Rasa Tarangini, Bhavaprakasha

**Therapeutic indications (classical):**
- *Prameha* (diabetes / urinary disorders) — primary indication
- *Mutra-roga* (urinary tract conditions)
- *Netra-roga* (eye disorders) — including cataracts and inflammatory eye conditions
- *Kushtha* (skin diseases) — chronic skin conditions
- *Pradara* (menorrhagia, leucorrhoea)
- *Vrishya* / *Vajikarana* — vitalizing and reproductive support
- *Rasayana* — rejuvenative effects

**Modern pharmacology research:**

Zinc-bhasma is among the best-characterized in modern peer-reviewed research:

- **Anti-diabetic activity**: Multiple animal studies show blood glucose reduction; zinc is a known cofactor for insulin function
- **Antimicrobial**: Strong activity confirmed against gram-positive (*S. aureus*, *S. mutans*) and gram-negative (*E. coli*, *P. aeruginosa*) bacteria; effective against fungi (*Candida*)
- **Anti-inflammatory**: documented in animal models
- **Wound healing**: zinc is essential for tissue repair; topical and systemic use
- **Anti-cancer**: preliminary studies show activity against various cancer cell lines
- **Anti-angiogenic**: yashada-bhasma research shows specific effects on neovascularization
- **Immunomodulatory**: zinc essentiality for immune function; bhasma form provides bioavailable zinc
- **Eye health**: classical indication confirmed by modern research showing zinc role in retinal function and macular health

**Dosage:** 30-125 mg twice daily classical; modern licensed Ayurvedic practice typically 60-125 mg daily under physician supervision

**Toxicity:** Zinc has wide safety margin compared to other dhatus:
- Adequate intake levels: 8-11 mg/day adult
- Chronic toxicity threshold: ~150-200 mg/day
- Acute toxicity: requires very high doses (>500 mg)
- Yashada-bhasma at typical doses is far below toxicity threshold
- IC50 in cell viability >1000 μg/mL — essentially non-toxic at any reasonable therapeutic dose

The toxicity profile is the most favorable of any dhatu-bhasma (rivaling iron, superior to all heavy-metal-based preparations).

### 3.2 Yashada-Sindura

**Composition:** Mercury-mediated zinc preparation containing HgS, ZnS, and intermediate phases  
**Use:** Specific therapeutic indications, particularly chronic skin conditions

This preparation involves Guru-graha (zinc) + Budha-graha (mercury) + Mangala-graha (sulfur). Cross-reference Volume VI §3.2 (vanga-sindura) for parallel chemistry and graha-tension analysis.

### 3.3 Brass Preparations (Riti / Pitala)

Brass is copper-zinc alloy (typically 60-90% Cu, 10-40% Zn). Classical Indian texts include brass preparations:
- *Riti-bhasma* — bhasma form of brass
- *Pitala* — alternative term for brass

Two-graha (Mangala for copper + Guru for zinc). Mangala-Guru is *friend* both directions — same favorable pair as bell-bronze and Aranmula (Volume V §4).

Brass has distinctive properties:
- More malleable than bronze
- Different acoustic properties (less harmonic richness than tin-bronze)
- Antimicrobial (zinc + copper synergistic)
- Used historically for vessels, decorative items, some musical instruments

The classical inclusion of brass preparations alongside copper-tin bronze preparations recognizes that both copper-zinc and copper-tin alloys produce useful materials with distinct properties.

### 3.4 Zinc in Sacred Alloys

Zinc appears as a component in **ashtadhatu** (8 metals, 12.5% by mass equal-weighted). Zinc is *not* in canonical panchaloha (5 metals: gold, silver, copper, tin, iron).

Zinc's contribution to ashtadhatu:
- Adds Guru-graha to the multi-metal cosmological integration
- Same Guru-friendship pattern as tin
- Two Guru-correspondence metals (zinc + tin) in ashtadhatu means strong Guru-presence in the alloy

The framework's structural validation finding (91.7% precision/recall for ashtadhatu) directly involves zinc as one of the 8 components. The dual Guru-presence may contribute to the strong validation result by creating coherent expansive influence in the multi-metal integration.
