# Extracting Classical Indian Music Cosmology Focused on Rāgas

## Executive summary

Classical Indian music theory links melodic forms (rāga-s and their predecessors) to a broader “cosmology” of affect (rasa), time, season, and divinity. In the source set assembled here, the **Nāṭyaśāstra** (Bharata) functions mainly as a *pre-rāga* foundation: it does **not** present a developed named-rāga corpus (e.g., Bhairav, Yaman) in the later sense; even the specific term **grāma-rāga** is reported as absent from the Nāṭyaśāstra in standard reference treatment. citeturn1search1 By contrast, **Śārṅgadeva’s Saṅgītaratnākara** (especially its rāga-discernment chapter) provides explicit mappings in which individual rāga-s are tied to **(i) time-slices of the day**, **(ii) seasons**, **(iii) specific rasas**, **(iv) presiding deities (including planets/nodes as well as deities such as Śiva and Viṣṇu)**, and **(v) dramaturgical/ritual contexts** (e.g., denouement *nirvahana*, “chamberlain enters,” festive occasions). citeturn21view1turn22view0turn34view0turn36view0

For later **Hindustani (North Indian) performance practice**, a modern canonical compendium (the *Rāga Guide*) explicitly affirms that a rāga’s association with an **emotional state, season, or time of day** is integral alongside melodic structure, and it documents a number of concrete “rāga–time” pairings (e.g., Bhairav at daybreak; Bhimpalasi early afternoon 12–3; Malkauns late night 12–3; Gaud Malhar “any time during the rainy season”), as well as deity-inflected interpretation in some cases (e.g., Bhairav as a name/form of Śiva). citeturn41view0turn41view2turn41view4

What follows is a compact but provenance-rigorous extraction of **rāga-level cosmological assertions** explicitly present in the cited passages. Where a field is not directly specified, it is marked **unspecified**.

## Source context and methodological notes

The extraction is constrained by what the sources *explicitly* state. The Nāṭyaśāstra is acknowledged as foundational to rasa-theory and early music theory, but standard reference treatment notes that the expression **grāma-rāga** is *not* found in it, and the named-rāga cosmologies typical of medieval and later traditions emerge more clearly in later treatises. citeturn1search1 Within the Saṅgītaratnākara tradition, the translation used here explicitly situates its rāga-discussion in relation to earlier systematic treatments (e.g., noting Mataṅga’s *Bṛhaddēśī* schema of grāma-rāga-s grouped under gīti-s), underscoring continuity in classificatory thinking. citeturn29view0

For Hindustani practice, the *Rāga Guide* treats “time/season/emotional state” as an essential dimension of rāga identity, and provides concrete time assignments for some rāga-s in its entries; those are extracted verbatim at the level of the “Time:” field and nearby semantic description. citeturn41view4
