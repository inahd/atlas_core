# Cut-and-Project Construction Choices per Nitya Devi

Each Nitya Devi's quasicrystal field uses an N-fold symmetry projection.
For non-standard N (those not in the crystallographic or well-studied
quasicrystal families), the construction choice is documented here.

## Well-established constructions (no choice needed)

- **N=3** (Kameshvari, Nityaklinna, Bherunda): triangular lattice. Periodic.
- **N=5** (not directly assigned, but Penrose tiling is the canonical 5-fold): icosahedral projection.
- **N=6** (Bhagamalini, Mahavajreshvari): hexagonal lattice. Periodic.
- **N=8** (Vahnivasini, Tvarita): Ammann-Beenker tiling. Well-established.
- **N=10** (Nitya): decagonal quasicrystal. Well-established from Al-Mn-Si alloys.
- **N=12** (Vijaya): dodecagonal quasicrystal. Well-established from soft-matter literature.

## Construction choices for non-standard N

### N=7 — Sivaduti (tithi 7)
Generalized Penrose / multigrid method with 7 equispaced plane waves.
The tradition describes Sivaduti as "Siva's messenger across the boundary."
The April 8 verification notes: "irrational aperiodic field, still bindu,
churning periphery." The 7-fold multigrid naturally produces this — a calm
center (where all 7 waves constructively interfere) surrounded by increasingly
complex aperiodic structure.

### N=9 — Kulasundari (tithi 9)
9-fold multigrid. The tradition describes her as "lineage transmission,
kula beauty." 9 = 3×3, and the field shows nested triangular motifs at
two scales — the lineage (3-fold) expressed through itself (3×3). This is
also the navamsha number, connecting her to the D9 divisional chart.

### N=11 — Nilapataka (tithi 11)
11-fold multigrid. This is the Ekadashi Nitya. The April 8 verification
found "85% Sri Yantra alignment at k=12.0." The 11-fold field is the
closest approach to the full 15-fold structure while remaining fundamentally
aperiodic. 11 is Jupiter's retrograde station count. Her rendering should
visibly approach Sri-Yantra-like structure without reaching it.

### N=13 — Sarvangasundari (tithi 13)
13-fold multigrid. The tradition describes her as "all-limbed beauty."
13 is prime; the field is irreducibly aperiodic. The construction uses
13 equispaced waves, producing a high-complexity pattern where no local
region repeats — "every limb is unique," matching her name.

### N=14 — Jvalamalini (tithi 14)
14-fold multigrid. The tradition describes her as "fire-garlanded,
transformation ring." 14 = 2×7, and the field shows 7-fold motifs with
a 2-fold reflection symmetry overlaid — fire (7, Mars) doubled into a
garland (ring, circle). She is the last Nitya before the full bloom.

### N=15 — Citra / Chidagni Kala (tithi 15)
Full 15-fold multigrid. "The variegated, all colors present." This is
the complete Sri Yantra structure — all 15 waves active simultaneously.
Her rendering IS the Sri Yantra field.

## Provisional constructions

All non-standard N use the multigrid method (N equispaced plane waves
with cos interference). This is mathematically well-defined for any N
but does not produce a substitution tiling with known tile shapes for
N=7, 9, 11, 13, 14. The multigrid fields are the Fourier-space dual
of the tiling and carry the same symmetry information.

If specific substitution-rule tilings become available for these N values,
they can replace the multigrid construction while keeping the same interface.

## 3D Polyhedron Mediators

Each Nitya Devi's 3D polyhedron is the intermediate object between
the higher-dimensional parent lattice and the 2D quasicrystal projection.
Its shadow IS the 2D Nitya render.

### Platonic/Archimedean mediators (standard N)

- **N=3** → tetrahedron (4 faces, simplest Platonic)
- **N=4** → cube (6 faces)
- **N=5** → icosahedron (20 faces, golden-ratio symmetry)
- **N=6** → cuboctahedron (14 faces, Archimedean)
- **N=10** → dodecahedron (12 pentagonal faces, dual of icosahedron)
- **N=12** → icosahedron (dodecagonal projection axis)
- **N=15** → rhombic triacontahedron (30 rhombic faces = 30 tithis)

### Antiprism mediators (non-standard N)

- **N=7** (Sivaduti) → heptagonal antiprism (14 triangular + 2 heptagonal faces).
  The 7-fold rotational axis is preserved. The antiprism's two heptagonal
  caps represent the two pakshas; the 14 triangular side faces represent
  the 14 intermediate tithis.

- **N=8** (Vahnivasini, Tvarita) → octagonal antiprism. 8-fold axis.
  Could alternatively use a truncated cube for closer Ammann-Beenker
  correspondence; antiprism chosen for consistency.

- **N=9** (Kulasundari) → nonagonal antiprism. 9 = navamsha. The 9-fold
  axis connects this to the D9 divisional chart.

- **N=11** (Nilapataka) → hendecagonal antiprism. 11-fold quasicrystalline
  axis. Jupiter's retrograde station count.

- **N=13** (Sarvangasundari) → 13-gonal antiprism. 13 is prime; the
  resulting polyhedron is irreducible.

- **N=14** (Jvalamalini) → 14-gonal antiprism. 14 = 2×7, producing a
  doubled-Mars structure.

### Whole-month polyhedron

The rhombic triacontahedron (30 rhombic faces = 60 triangular faces in pairs)
is the resting-state view. Each rhombic face is labeled with a tithi number
(1-30). Pairs of triangles sharing a dodecahedron-type edge (T-T edge) form
one rhombus. Both triangles in a pair carry the same face label so they
highlight together.

Constructed as the dual of the icosidodecahedron: the 32 vertices are the
face centers of the icosidodecahedron (12 from pentagonal faces = 5-valent
icosahedron-type, 20 from triangular faces = 3-valent dodecahedron-type).
All 32 vertices lie on the convex hull.

Face ordering: sorted by centroid azimuthal angle for deterministic
tithi-to-face assignment. This means adjacent tithis map to nearby faces
on the polyhedron, approximating the lunar cycle's angular progression.
