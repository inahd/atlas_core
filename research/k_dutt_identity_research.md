# K. Dutt Identity — 1914 Navadvipa Panjika

*Research note for atlas_core. Resolution of the unresolved bibliographic question from prior Gaudiya astronomical lineage research (April 2026 chat).*

---

## The question

The April 2026 Gaudiya astronomical lineage research identified an unresolved figure: a co-editor or contributor to the 1914 *Navadvipa Panjika* identified in some sources as "K. Dutt." The previous research note flagged this as "a strong inference but not a documented fact" — specifically the inference that "K. Dutt" was Bhaktivinoda Thakura. This note revisits the question with current sources.

---

## The 1914 Navadvipa Panjika — what it actually is

**Origin.** Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati (1874–1937), born Bimala Prasad Datta as the son of Bhaktivinoda Thakura, composed an early version of the *Navadvipa Panjika* at age 12 (~1886) at the request of Jagannatha Dasa Babaji. This childhood version was an astrological almanac in which months, lunar fortnights, days, lunar days, and stars were given names of Vishnu — described in one source as a "Krishnaized almanac."

The 1914 publication is a developed, mature form of this same calendar tradition, formally titled *Navadvīpa-pañjikā* (alongside *Bhaktibhāvana-pañjikā*) in Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati's bibliography. Wikipedia's bibliography of Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati lists it directly under his original works. ISKCON's GBC and the contemporary Mayapur calendar tradition cite Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati as the established compiler/founder of the *Visuddha Sri Navadvipa Panjika* (Mayapur Calendar) tradition.

**Mathematical basis.** The calendar is grounded in *Surya-Siddhanta* astronomical methodology, with calculation of dates following detailed instructions in *Sri Hari-bhakti-vilasa* (Sanatana Goswami) and other Vaishnava-granthas. The five-part panjika structure (*pancha-anga*: tithi, paksha, nakshatra, yoga, karana, vaara) is standard panchanga structure refined for Gaudiya use.

**Function.** The calendar provides dates for religious festivals, appearance and disappearance days of devotees and acharyas, Ekadashi vrata calculations, and ritually important events. It is still in continuous use and informs the present-day ISKCON/Gaudiya calendar systems.

## What "K. Dutt" likely refers to

**Bhaktivinoda Thakura's birth name was Kedarnath Datta** (Bengali: Kedāra-nātha Datta, "Kedarnath Dutt" in colonial transliteration). He was born 2 September 1838 in the village of Ula (now Birnagar), Bengal. He served in the British Raj Judicial Service as a Deputy Magistrate, retiring in 1894. The name "Kedarnath Dutt" appears in colonial-era documents — Hindu College attendance registers, government service records, the Hindu Herald, and 19th-century newspaper notices regarding the Gaur Vishnupriya Temple Fund (where he is referenced as "Babu Kedar Nath Dutt, the distinguished Deputy Magistrate"). Bipin Bihari Goswami's testimonial granting him the title "Bhaktivinoda" was issued in 1881, but Kedarnath continued to use "Kedarnath Dutt" in many official and publishing contexts after that.

**The "K. Dutt" attribution as Kedarnath Dutt = Bhaktivinoda Thakura is therefore a well-supported biographical inference**, not just a guess. The transliteration "K. Dutt" or "K. Datta" for Kedarnath Datta/Dutt is standard for the period.

**The complication.** Bhaktivinoda Thakura died on **23 June 1914**. The 1914 *Navadvipa Panjika* was published in or for that calendar year. Whether the publication preceded his death (in which case he was alive during preparation but in advanced infirmity, having been bedridden during the 1911 Balighai conference period and in formal samadhi-like state since 1910) or followed his death (in which case it was a posthumous publication crediting him as co-editor by lineage authority) is the actual unresolved question.

**The likely answer.** The 1914 publication appears to be Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati's compilation, with Bhaktivinoda Thakura's name attached in editorial-credit position because:

1. The calendar's principles derive from Bhaktivinoda's broader work establishing the Mayapur tradition (rediscovery of Mahaprabhu's birthplace, establishment of Yogapitha, etc.).
2. Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati had an explicit pattern across his publications of editing his father's works — the bibliography lists numerous works "edited by Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakur" of works originally authored by Bhaktivinoda.
3. The 1914 *Bhagavad-Gita* publication explicitly carries this dual attribution: "with annotation by Srila Vishvanath Cakravarty and translation by Srila Thakur Bhaktivinoda" — showing Bhaktisiddhanta's standard practice in 1914 of crediting his father's contributions.
4. The Vaishnava convention of co-attributing devotional publications to spiritual lineage rather than to single authors makes joint editorship/credit standard for material developed within an acharya's tradition.

So: "K. Dutt" = Kedarnath Dutt = Bhaktivinoda Thakura, with high confidence. The co-editorial attribution reflects the actual collaborative nature of the calendar's intellectual development across father-son lineage, with Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati doing the technical-astronomical work and Bhaktivinoda Thakura's broader Gaudiya cosmological framework providing the devotional structure.

## What remains genuinely uncertain

**The specific 1914 publication's credit page.** Without holding a physical copy of the 1914 *Navadvipa Panjika* (held at the Bhaktivedanta Research Centre Kolkata or other archives, mostly undigitized), the exact credit-line wording cannot be confirmed. Whether it reads "by Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati and K. Dutt," "by Bhaktivinoda Thakura," "edited by Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati from materials by Kedarnath Dutt Bhaktivinoda," or some other formulation is not visible from secondary sources.

**Whether the Vishnu-name overlay tables** (tithi → Vishnu name, nakshatra → Vishnu name, weekday → Vishnu name) are documented in a separate textual layer. These were the specific feature flagged in the prior research as not having an English translation. They may be present in:

- The 1914 *Navadvipa Panjika* itself as section content
- A separate companion publication
- The original 1886 childhood version Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati prepared for Jagannatha Dasa Babaji
- *Hari-bhakti-vilasa* itself, with the Panjika applying rather than originating the overlay

**Whether any K. Dutt other than Kedarnath Dutt could be intended.** The Datta/Dutt surname is common in Bengal kayastha families. Without seeing the source documents, the possibility of confusion with another K. Dutt cannot be fully excluded. However, no other plausible candidate appears in the Gaudiya intellectual lineage of that period for whom co-editorial association with Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati's calendar-work would make sense. The strong contextual fit with Kedarnath Dutt is hard to displace.

## Where to look for definitive answer

The 1914 *Navadvipa Panjika*'s actual title page and credit page would resolve the question definitively. Likely locations:

**Bhaktivedanta Research Centre, Kolkata.** The major holding institution for Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati's surviving manuscripts and early printed editions. The previous Gaudiya astronomical lineage research identified this archive as the location of most undigitized Bhaktisiddhanta corpus material. Direct contact with the Centre to request scans of the 1914 Panjika title page would be the highest-yield move.

**Bhagavata Press archives.** Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati's "Bhāgavat Yantrālaya" press at 4 Sānagar Lane, Kālighāṭ, Calcutta, established 1913, was the publication apparatus through which the 1914 works passed. After Bhaktivinoda's death in June 1914, the press relocated to Sri Vrajapattan at Mayapur in January 1915. Press records, if preserved, would document the 1914 Panjika's preparation and publication.

**British Library / India Office Records.** Colonial-era publications often had legal-deposit copies in British library collections. Worth checking for a 1914 Bengali-language calendar publication catalogued under either Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati's name or under Kedarnath Dutt's.

**Sundarananda Vidyavinoda's biography of Bhaktivinoda Thakura.** The principal early biographical source on Bhaktivinoda. May contain references to the 1914 calendar publication's preparation circumstances given Bhaktivinoda's terminal illness during that period.

**Ferdinando Sardella's *Modern Hindu Personalism*** (Oxford UP, 2013, reprinted). The major contemporary academic biography of Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati. Sardella's footnotes and references would likely cite specific archival sources for the 1914 publications. This is the single highest-yield secondary source for resolving the question without direct archival access.

**Bhakti Vikasa Swami's *Sri Bhaktisiddhanta Vaibhava*** (2009). Devotional biography with extensive bibliographic detail. Less rigorous than Sardella but more detailed on individual publications.

## Resolution status

**Functionally resolved.** "K. Dutt" in the 1914 *Navadvipa Panjika* context refers to Kedarnath Dutt = Bhaktivinoda Thakura. This identification is consistent with the broader publishing pattern of the Bhagavata Press in 1913–1914, with Bhaktivinoda's biographical name and recognition in the broader Gaudiya tradition, and with the absence of any plausible alternative candidate.

**Definitively resolved only with direct archival access.** The exact wording of the 1914 publication's credit page would close the question completely. This requires either Bhaktivedanta Research Centre access in Kolkata, or careful reading of Sardella (2013) and Bhakti Vikasa Swami (2009) for specific archival citations they may have included.

For Atlas's purposes — the lineage research can proceed treating "K. Dutt" as Bhaktivinoda Thakura with high confidence. The remaining uncertainty is the kind that affects bibliographic citation precision rather than the substantive understanding of the lineage.

## Note on the broader Vishnu-name overlay question

The previous research flagged that the full Vishnu-name overlay tables (tithi → Vishnu name; nakshatra → Vishnu name; weekday → Vishnu name) appear to exist only in Bengali/Sanskrit, with no located English translation. This separate question (independent of the K. Dutt identity) remains open.

The overlay system is rooted in *Hari-bhakti-vilasa* by Sanatana Goswami (16th century), which contains specifications for daily Vishnu-name remembrance correlated to time-cycle positions. Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati's *Navadvipa Panjika* applies and operationalizes these specifications in calendar form. The full tables in their applied calendar form may be documented in the 1914 publication itself or in subsequent editions.

**Concrete next move on this question:** Request scans or PDF of the 1914 *Navadvipa Panjika* (or any subsequent edition reprinting the same material) from Bhaktivedanta Research Centre, Kolkata. If the Vishnu-name overlay tables are present in the publication, they could be directly transcribed and translated to populate Atlas's relational graph with the specific time-cycle to Vishnu-name correspondences. This would be a substantive addition to the kala-domain dataset.

## Summary

"K. Dutt" = Kedarnath Dutt = Bhaktivinoda Thakura. Resolution is functional with high confidence based on biographical convergence; definitive resolution requires direct archival access to the 1914 publication's title page. The deeper question — full Vishnu-name overlay tables in English — remains genuinely open and would benefit from direct contact with Bhaktivedanta Research Centre or careful citation-tracing through Sardella (2013) and Bhakti Vikasa Swami (2009).

For atlas_core: the K. Dutt question can be marked resolved-pending-archival-confirmation. The Vishnu-name overlay tables remain a real outstanding research target with clear next steps.
