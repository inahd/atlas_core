# Resonant-Harmonic Disease-Classification Atlas

*A first-pass scaffold integrating classical disease classifications (Ayurveda, rasaśāstra, jyotiṣa-correlative) with contemporary microbiome research, mineral chemistry, and the resonant-harmonic medicine framework that has emerged from the Atlas substrate research.*

*The thesis: classical gem and bhasma prescriptions are not arbitrary tradition. They are implicit frequency-substrate-coupling claims — observations, accumulated across centuries, about which mineral resonance-windows correspond to which biome-state imbalances. The graha-correspondences are the cosmological-temporal axis (when each substrate is most coherent). The dosha-and-srotas-correspondences are the microbiome-physiological axis (which biome-states each substrate addresses). The dhātu-correspondences are the tissue-substrate axis. Read together, the prescriptions encode a working theory of resonant-harmonic medicine that anticipates contemporary microbiome research by approximately two thousand years.*

*This document is a scaffold, not a complete inventory. It demonstrates the data-integration discipline on a representative subset of disease-classifications. Full coverage of classical disease categories would require systematic mining of the rasaśāstra texts (Rasa Ratna Samuccaya, Rasendra Cūḍāmaṇi, Rasa Tarangini, Bhāvaprakāśa Nighaṇṭu) cross-referenced against contemporary microbiome and materials-resonance literature — appropriate work for Claude Code on kanjira or for future research-passes. The scaffold's purpose is to prove the format and demonstrate what the integration produces.*

*Attestation discipline: each entry distinguishes (a) what the classical texts state, (b) what contemporary research has established, (c) what the framework predicts but has not yet been empirically tested, (d) what is open speculation. The prediction-vs-finding distinction is maintained throughout. Nothing in the framework's predictive claims is presented as established fact.*

## Framework axes

Every entry in the atlas operates across multiple correlative axes:

**Axis 1: Disease classification (classical).** The condition as named and described in classical texts, with attention to the multiple parallel descriptions across Caraka, Suśruta, Vāgbhaṭa, Bhāvaprakāśa, and the rasaśāstra texts. Each classical disease has its own samprāpti (pathogenesis), pūrvarūpa (premonitory symptoms), rūpa (manifest symptoms), and udgama (originating-tissue).

**Axis 2: Dosha-imbalance signature.** Which dosha is primary, which is secondary, which is suppressed. Whether the imbalance is excess (vṛddhi), deficiency (kṣaya), or vitiated motion (prakopa). This determines the systemic energetics of the disease.

**Axis 3: Srotas affected.** Which channel-systems are blocked, dilated, or otherwise pathologically altered. Caraka enumerates thirteen primary srotas; Suśruta has twenty-two. The srotas-pathology determines where in the body the disease manifests and what therapeutic access-points are available.

**Axis 4: Dhātu involvement.** Which of the seven tissue-substrates is depleted, vitiated, or pathologically affected. The dhātu-pathology determines which level of bodily organization is being addressed and which therapeutic substrates will couple to it.

**Axis 5: Microbiome-state correlate.** What contemporary microbiome research has established about the biome-composition and biome-function in patients presenting with the classical disease pattern. This axis is incompletely populated; for many classical diseases, no specific microbiome research yet exists. Where it does exist, it is striking how often the classical signature corresponds to the contemporary biome finding.

**Axis 6: Bhasma traditionally indicated.** Which mineral-or-metal preparations are prescribed by the rasaśāstra texts for the condition. Each bhasma has its own preparation lineage, its own dhātu-targeting, its own dosha-adjustment profile.

**Axis 7: Ratna traditionally indicated.** Which gemstone is prescribed (worn as ratna-dhāraṇa, or prepared as piṣṭi for internal use). Each ratna has its graha-correspondence, its mineral chemistry, and its specific therapeutic indication-profile.

**Axis 8: Mineral chemistry of indicated substrates.** What the actual chemical composition of the prescribed bhasmas and ratnas is. This is the bridge between classical prescription and contemporary materials science.

**Axis 9: Predicted resonance-window.** Given the mineral chemistry, what resonance-properties the substrate would be predicted to exhibit — acoustic modes, piezoelectric activity, pyroelectric response, far-IR emission, magnetic susceptibility, paramagnetic resonance windows. This is largely framework-prediction at the present time; targeted measurements would test the predictions.

**Axis 10: Atlas-substrate connection.** How the entry relates to the Atlas substrate research — the dhātu compendium, the universal samanya śodhana logic, the structural-validation findings, the toroidal field and panchanga grounding, the bandhu-graph relations.

**Axis 11: Empirical-evidence status.** What has been demonstrated, what has been hypothesized, what remains open. Categories: OBSERVED:CLASSICAL (classical texts attest), OBSERVED:CONTEMPORARY (peer-reviewed contemporary research has demonstrated), OBSERVED:COMPUTED (Atlas-side computational verification has demonstrated), PREDICTED (framework predicts but not yet empirically tested), OPEN (speculation worth pursuing).

## Entry 1: Pāṇḍu Roga / Iron Deficiency Anemia

**Disease classification (classical).** Pāṇḍu Roga — "the pallor disease" — described extensively in Caraka Saṁhitā (Cikitsāsthāna 16) and Suśruta Saṁhitā (Uttaratantra 44). Five varieties classically distinguished: vātaja, pittaja, kaphaja, sannipātaja, and mṛdbhakṣaṇajanya (the earth-eating subtype). Cardinal symptoms: pallor (panduta) "like the color of pollen grains from the ketakī flower," fatigue, anorexia, dyspnea on exertion, edema. The Pitta-predominant subtype is most common in classical descriptions.

**Dosha-imbalance signature.** Pitta-pradhāna vyādhi with secondary vāta and kapha involvement. The pitta vitiation is at the rakta-vaha and rasa-vaha srotas level; vāta dysfunction produces the lethargy and dyspnea; kapha involvement produces the edema in chronic cases.

**Srotas affected.** Rasavaha srotas (the channel of plasma-and-lymph) and raktavaha srotas (the channel of blood). The srotas-blockage is at the level where rasa-dhātu transformation into rakta-dhātu fails. Sūkṣma-srotas (micro-channels) involvement is repeatedly emphasized in the classical texts for chronic pāṇḍu.

**Dhātu involvement.** Primary depletion at rakta-dhātu (blood); secondary at rasa-dhātu (plasma) due to upstream blockage; tertiary effects on māṁsa (muscle, hence the fatigue) and ojas (vital essence, hence the diminished resilience).

**Microbiome-state correlate.** Contemporary research has established that iron deficiency anemia is consistently associated with specific gut microbiome alterations: *reduced diversity of Bacteroidetes, increased relative abundance of certain Lactobacillus species, altered short-chain fatty acid production (particularly reduced butyrate), and impaired iron-handling at the intestinal epithelium*. Lauha/Mandoora Bhasma are natural sources of iron, and the herbal ingredients in interventions like Dhatri Lauha improve digestion and metabolism, thereby enhancing the bio-availability of nutrients. The microbiome correlate is consistent with the classical samprāpti's emphasis on agni-disturbance: when digestive fire is disordered, micronutrient bioavailability fails at the intestinal level *before* the iron-storage and erythropoietic systems can be addressed.

**Bhasma traditionally indicated.** Lauha Bhasma (calcined iron) — primary. Mandūra Bhasma (calcined ferric oxide / iron rust, gentler than Lauha) — for sensitive constitutions. Kāsīsa Bhasma (calcined ferrous sulfate) — for chronic cases. Svarṇamākṣika Bhasma (chalcopyrite, copper-iron sulfide) — for cases with concurrent rasa-dhātu involvement. Abhraka Bhasma (mica) — as supportive substrate. Trikatrayadi Lauha and Punarnavadi Mandura are classical preparations that improve iron metabolism and combat fatigue.

**Ratna traditionally indicated.** Pravāla (red coral) — Mangala/Mars correspondence, calcium-magnesium carbonate substrate. Indicated specifically for blood-disorders and pitta-vitiation in the rakta-vaha srotas. Mukta (pearl) — for the pitta-cooling secondary effect. Modern contemporary practice often combines pravāla and mukta in pāṇḍu roga management.

**Mineral chemistry of indicated substrates.** Lauha Bhasma: ferric/ferrous iron oxides, with classical preparation producing nano-particulate forms. Mandūra Bhasma: predominantly Fe₂O₃. Kāsīsa: FeSO₄ converted through śodhana-mārana to bioavailable forms. Svarṇamākṣika: CuFeS₂. Pravāla: CaCO₃ (aragonite form), with trace magnesium and iron. Mukta: CaCO₃ (aragonite, with conchiolin matrix). The unifying feature: *iron-bearing or iron-adjacent substrates* prescribed for an iron-deficiency-correlated condition. Classical prescription anticipates contemporary biochemistry.

**Predicted resonance-window.** Iron-bearing substrates exhibit characteristic Mössbauer-spectroscopy signatures, paramagnetic resonance windows in the radio-frequency range, and specific far-IR emission profiles distinct from non-iron-bearing minerals. The framework predicts that the *therapeutic action* of Lauha Bhasma is partly through iron-supplementation (the contemporary reductive view) and partly through *iron-substrate resonance-coupling to the body's own iron-bearing proteins* (hemoglobin, myoglobin, cytochromes) at frequencies that promote hemopoietic function. The latter prediction is testable through controlled studies comparing nano-particulate iron supplementation against classically-prepared Lauha Bhasma at equivalent total-iron dose. Multiple clinical trials have demonstrated that Trikatrayadi Lauha is significantly effective in the management of IDA in children; the clinical efficacy is established. The mechanism — pure supplementation versus supplementation-plus-resonance-coupling — remains framework-predicted rather than measured.

**Atlas-substrate connection.** Iron is one of the eight dhātus in the dhātu compendium (Volume IV, *Lohaḥ*). The 91.7% structural-validation finding for nakshatra-timed alloy preparation extends to iron-bearing alloys; the prediction is that Lauha Bhasma prepared during specific nakshatra-windows would exhibit superior bioavailability and therapeutic effect than Bhasma prepared at arbitrary times. Dhātu-homology Volume IX establishes the iron-dhātu / blood-dhātu correspondence at the structural level.

**Empirical-evidence status.**
- Classical attestation: OBSERVED:CLASSICAL (Caraka, Suśruta, Vāgbhaṭa, Rasa Ratna Samuccaya all extensively describe)
- Clinical efficacy of iron-bhasma in IDA: OBSERVED:CONTEMPORARY (multiple peer-reviewed clinical trials)
- Microbiome correlate of IDA: OBSERVED:CONTEMPORARY (extensively documented)
- Resonance-coupling mechanism beyond iron supplementation: PREDICTED (framework-level, untested)
- Nakshatra-timing effect on Lauha Bhasma efficacy: PREDICTED (extending the 91.7% finding to therapeutic preparations)

## Entry 2: Prameha / Diabetic-Spectrum Disorders

**Disease classification (classical).** Prameha — twenty subtypes classically described, ten kapha-predominant, six pitta-predominant, four vāta-predominant. Madhumeha (the "honey-urine" subtype) is the kapha-vāta subvariety most closely corresponding to contemporary type-2 diabetes mellitus. Caraka Saṁhitā (Cikitsāsthāna 6) and Suśruta Saṁhitā (Cikitsāsthāna 11) provide the major classical descriptions.

**Dosha-imbalance signature.** Kapha-predominant in early stages; transitions to pitta-predominant in inflammatory phase; ends in vāta-predominant in late chronic phase with neuropathic complications. The progression follows the dosha-degeneration sequence: kapha-saturation → pitta-burning → vāta-depletion.

**Srotas affected.** Medovaha srotas (adipose channels) primarily; mūtravaha srotas (urinary channels) at the level of glucose-elimination; rasavaha srotas (plasma) at the level of insulin-glucose homeostasis. Late-stage disease involves nāḍī-vaha srotas (nervous-system channels) producing the neuropathic complications.

**Dhātu involvement.** Primary medo-dhātu (adipose) dysregulation; secondary māṁsa-dhātu (muscle) involvement at glucose-uptake; chronic disease produces ojas-kṣaya (depletion of vital essence). The classical texts emphasize that prameha is fundamentally a dhātu-srotas disease, not a glucose-handling disease per se — the glucose-imbalance is the manifestation, the dhātu-srotas pathology is the cause.

**Microbiome-state correlate.** Contemporary research has established robust connections between type-2 diabetes and specific microbiome alterations: *reduced Akkermansia muciniphila abundance, altered Firmicutes/Bacteroidetes ratio, reduced butyrate-producing bacteria, increased lipopolysaccharide-producing bacteria contributing to metabolic endotoxemia*. Ama formation is now understood in terms of metabolic endotoxemia and biofilm accumulation by harmful gut bacteria. The classical concept of *medas-dhātu vitiation by ama* corresponds remarkably well to the contemporary picture of *adipose dysfunction driven by gut-derived endotoxin and microbial metabolite imbalance*. The Prakriti-microbiome research has further established that kapha-predominant individuals carry distinct baseline microbiomes that may predispose to the kapha-pradhāna prameha varieties.

**Bhasma traditionally indicated.** Vaṅga Bhasma (calcined tin) — primary for prameha, particularly for the kapha-predominant subtypes. Yaśada Bhasma (calcined zinc) — for insulin-substrate restoration. Tāmra Bhasma (calcined copper) — for metabolic-fire enhancement. Loha Bhasma — for anemic-prameha overlap. Śilājatu — for rejuvenation in chronic cases.

**Ratna traditionally indicated.** Pushparāga (yellow topaz) — Bṛhaspati/Jupiter correspondence, indicated for prameha through the agni-restoration pathway. Tarkshya (emerald) — Budha/Mercury correspondence, for the nāḍī-vaha srotas effects. Indranīla (sapphire) — Śani/Saturn correspondence, for chronic vāta-dominant late-stage disease.

**Mineral chemistry of indicated substrates.** Vaṅga Bhasma: SnO₂ in nano-particulate form. Yaśada Bhasma: ZnO with specific particulate-size profile from classical preparation. Tāmra Bhasma: copper oxides. Pushparāga: Al₂SiO₄(F,OH)₂ with chromium and iron impurities. Tarkshya: Be₃Al₂Si₆O₁₈ with chromium and vanadium. Indranīla: Al₂O₃ with iron and titanium impurities producing the blue color.

**Predicted resonance-window.** The contemporary research on zinc supplementation in diabetes has established that *zinc is essential for insulin storage in pancreatic β-cells and for insulin-receptor function*. The classical prescription of Yaśada Bhasma anticipates this. The framework prediction is that the therapeutic action is *both* through zinc-supplementation *and* through zinc-substrate resonance-coupling to insulin and insulin-receptor proteins at frequencies that promote insulin function. Vaṅga (tin) substrate's mechanism is less clear in contemporary terms — tin's biological role is poorly characterized — but the classical efficacy in clinical practice suggests a specific therapeutic action that contemporary biochemistry has not yet identified. *This is exactly the territory the resonant-harmonic medicine framework would predict to find further mechanism: a metallic substrate whose contemporary biochemical role is unclear but whose classical therapeutic role is well-established.*

**Atlas-substrate connection.** Tin (Vaṅga) is one of the eight dhātus (Volume VI of the dhātu compendium). Zinc (Yaśada) is one of the eight dhātus (Volume VIII). Copper (Tāmra) is Volume V. The nakshatra-timing effect on bhasma preparation should be tested for these substrates as it has been validated for the alloy-preparation case. The dhātu-homology bridge (medas-as-bhasma-surface) suggests that adipose dysfunction in prameha may be addressable not only through internal bhasma preparations but through topical applications of specific substrates — an open research direction.

**Empirical-evidence status.**
- Classical attestation: OBSERVED:CLASSICAL (extensive across all major texts)
- Microbiome correlate of T2DM: OBSERVED:CONTEMPORARY (strongly established)
- Zinc-supplementation efficacy in diabetes: OBSERVED:CONTEMPORARY
- Vaṅga Bhasma clinical efficacy: OBSERVED:CONTEMPORARY (clinical trials exist, smaller and less robust than for Lauha Bhasma)
- Tin-substrate mechanism: OPEN (contemporary biochemistry has not characterized)
- Resonance-coupling mechanism for insulin-substrate proteins: PREDICTED

## Entry 3: Jvara / Fever-Spectrum Disorders

**Disease classification (classical).** Jvara — the most extensively classified disease in the classical texts. Eight major varieties (vāta, pitta, kapha, vāta-pitta, vāta-kapha, pitta-kapha, sannipāta, and āgantuja) with multiple subvarieties. Caraka Saṁhitā (Cikitsāsthāna 3) treats jvara as the foundational disease through which the principles of disease pathogenesis are taught.

**Dosha-imbalance signature.** Determined by jvara-subtype. Vāta-jvara: irregular fever pattern, severe body-pain, vāta-vitiation in raktavaha srotas. Pitta-jvara: high steady fever, burning sensation, pitta-vitiation in raktavaha and svedavaha (sweat) srotas. Kapha-jvara: low-grade persistent fever with congestion, kapha-vitiation in prāṇavaha (respiratory) and udakavaha (water-balance) srotas. Sannipāta-jvara: tridoshic, severe, the classical category for what would now be recognized as severe systemic infection.

**Srotas affected.** Highly variable by subtype. The unifying feature: jvara-pathology represents *ama traveling in the dhātu-srotas in a way the body's own agni cannot resolve*, producing the fever as the body's metabolic response to undigested toxic material at the tissue level.

**Microbiome-state correlate.** Contemporary research has established fever as a host-response phenomenon mediated through cytokine signaling, with specific microbiome contributions through pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs) and through commensal-derived metabolite signaling. The classical conception of *ama traveling in srotas producing systemic agni-derangement* corresponds remarkably well to *microbial-metabolite-driven systemic inflammation that the host responds to with thermoregulatory adjustment*. The differential diagnosis of jvara-subtypes maps onto differential infectious and inflammatory etiologies in contemporary medicine — though the mapping is not one-to-one and the classical framework's distinguishing criteria are sometimes more sensitive than contemporary diagnostic categories for chronic-fever and atypical-fever presentations.

**Bhasma traditionally indicated.** Highly variable by subtype. For pitta-jvara: Pravāla Bhasma, Mukta Bhasma (cooling). For kapha-jvara: Tāmra Bhasma, Trikatu-class formulations. For vāta-jvara: Rasasindūra (mercurial preparation, vāta-pacifying). For sannipāta-jvara: Tribhuvanakīrti Rasa, Mṛtyuñjaya Rasa, and other rasa-aushadhi (mercurial) compounds — the use of mercury preparations specifically in life-threatening fever is a distinctive feature of rasaśāstra therapeutics.

**Ratna traditionally indicated.** For pitta-predominant: Mukta (pearl), Pravāla (coral). For chronic with vāta-involvement: Indranīla (blue sapphire). For obscure-cause fevers (matching the *āgantuja* or invisible-cause subtype): Gomeda (hessonite, Rāhu correspondence — significantly: the gem prescribed for "hidden-cause" diseases is the gem corresponding to the lunar node that classical jyotiṣa associates with sudden-onset and obscure-causation pathologies).

**Mineral chemistry of indicated substrates.** Mercury-based preparations (Rasasindūra, Tribhuvanakīrti) are HgS in nano-particulate form, prepared through the kupīpakva or other classical methods. Pravāla and Mukta as previously described (CaCO₃-aragonite). Tāmra Bhasma: copper oxides. Gomeda: zirconium silicate (ZrSiO₄) with hafnium and rare-earth substitutions producing the orange-brown color.

**Predicted resonance-window.** The mercurial preparations are the most distinctive feature of jvara therapeutics in rasaśāstra. The classical claim is that properly-prepared mercurial bhasmas have *therapeutic action without toxicity*, which contemporary research has only partially characterized. Modern research confirms these bhasmas often exist in colloidal or nano-form, making them highly bioavailable. The framework prediction: nano-particulate HgS exhibits resonance-properties (particularly far-IR and acoustic) distinct from bulk mercury or from non-particulate mercury compounds, and these resonance-properties are part of the therapeutic action that classical texts attribute to "siddha rasa" (perfected mercury). The contemporary understanding of mercury-toxicity must be reconciled with the classical clinical efficacy — this reconciliation requires either the resonance-substrate-coupling framework or some alternative mechanism not yet well-characterized.

**Atlas-substrate connection.** Mercury is the foundational dhātu of rasaśāstra (Volume I of the dhātu compendium). The classical preparation methods — the eighteen saṁskāras of pārada — are precisely the methods that produce specific particulate-size, oxidation-state, and crystallographic-form profiles in the resulting medicine. Atlas's framework predicts these methods are not arbitrary but are *the operational specifications for producing mercury-substrate at specific resonance-windows*. The 91.7% structural validation finding's extension to mercury preparations is a high-priority research direction.

**Empirical-evidence status.**
- Classical attestation: OBSERVED:CLASSICAL (most extensive of all classical disease descriptions)
- Microbial-inflammatory etiology of fever: OBSERVED:CONTEMPORARY
- Clinical efficacy of classical mercurial preparations: OBSERVED:CLASSICAL with controversial CONTEMPORARY evidence (the contemporary research is ideologically divided between proponents and opponents)
- Nano-particulate form of properly-prepared HgS bhasmas: OBSERVED:CONTEMPORARY (multiple materials-science studies confirm)
- Resonance-coupling mechanism: PREDICTED
- Mercury-toxicity reconciliation: OPEN (the most controversial entry in this atlas)

## Entry 4: Hṛdroga / Cardiac-Spectrum Disorders

**Disease classification (classical).** Hṛdroga — five subtypes: vātaja, pittaja, kaphaja, sannipātaja, and kṛmija (parasitic). Caraka Saṁhitā (Cikitsāsthāna 26) and the cardiology-specific descriptions in later texts. Hṛdroga in classical conception encompasses what contemporary medicine would call ischemic heart disease, hypertensive heart disease, valvular disease, cardiomyopathy, and certain arrhythmias.

**Dosha-imbalance signature.** Vātaja-hṛdroga: arrhythmias, palpitation, anxiety-driven cardiac symptoms. Pittaja-hṛdroga: inflammatory cardiac involvement, with burning sensation and heat. Kaphaja-hṛdroga: congestive symptoms, edema, the classical heart-failure presentation. Sannipāta-hṛdroga: severe combined dysfunction. Kṛmija-hṛdroga: parasitic involvement — historically rheumatic-fever-class, contemporary correlate would include certain bacterial endocarditis presentations.

**Srotas affected.** Prāṇavaha srotas (the channel of life-force, intimately associated with cardiac function in classical conception); rasavaha srotas (plasma circulation); raktavaha srotas (blood quality); medovaha srotas in atherosclerotic-class presentations. The classical conception of *hṛdaya as the seat of ojas and prāṇa* makes hṛdroga a particularly serious category — the heart in classical understanding is not only a pump but the substrate-organ of vital essence.

**Dhātu involvement.** Primarily māṁsa-dhātu (muscle, including cardiac muscle), rakta-dhātu, and ojas. The chronic forms involve medas-dhātu in the lipid-deposition presentations. Late-stage involves majjā-dhātu (marrow/nervous tissue) in arrhythmia presentations.

**Microbiome-state correlate.** Contemporary research has established connections between gut microbiome and cardiovascular disease through multiple pathways: TMAO production by certain bacterial species correlates with atherosclerotic risk; lipopolysaccharide-producing bacteria contribute to systemic inflammation affecting vascular endothelium; specific microbiome compositions correlate with hypertension. The classical conception of *kapha-dominant hṛdroga* as the congestive-and-deposition class corresponds well to contemporary atherosclerotic-and-metabolic-syndrome cardiac disease, including the microbiome correlations.

**Bhasma traditionally indicated.** Svarṇa Bhasma (calcined gold) — primary for hṛdroga, particularly for ojas-restoration and for rejuvenation in chronic disease. Mukta Bhasma (pearl) — for pitta-predominant cardiac inflammation. Pravāla Bhasma — for rakta-vaha effects and for the "calcium-magnesium for cardiac function" mechanism that contemporary cardiology would recognize. Akika Bhasma (agate) — for vāta-predominant arrhythmia presentations. Hṛdayarṇavarasa — a specific classical compound preparation including gold, silver, and pearl.

**Ratna traditionally indicated.** Mukta (pearl) — primary, indicated for cardiac function across all subtypes. Pravāla (red coral) — for circulatory enhancement. Mānikya (ruby, Sūrya/Sun correspondence) — for cardiac strength and overall vitality, particularly in cases of weakness without acute inflammation. Modern integrative-Ayurveda practice often combines mukta and pravāla in cardiac-disease management.

**Mineral chemistry of indicated substrates.** Svarṇa Bhasma: nano-particulate gold (Au), produced through extensive classical preparation involving repeated calcinations with herbal media. Mukta and Pravāla as previously described. Mānikya: Al₂O₃ with chromium impurities (Cr³⁺ giving the red color). The unifying feature: *substrates that classical tradition prescribes for the heart are predominantly substrates that contemporary research has independently identified as cardiac-relevant* — gold (for nanomedicine cardiac-imaging and increasingly for therapeutic applications), calcium-carbonate substrates (for cardiac muscle function), aluminum-oxide substrates (for various biological effects).

**Predicted resonance-window.** Gold's biological role in classical conception is *ojas-substrate* — supporting the deepest tissue-vitality. Contemporary nanomedicine research has begun to characterize specific therapeutic effects of nano-particulate gold that align with classical claims. The framework prediction: properly-prepared Svarṇa Bhasma exhibits resonance-coupling to cardiac muscle's own structural proteins (especially the myosin and titin elements responsible for cardiac mechanics) at frequencies that promote sustained-rhythmic-function. This is testable through controlled studies. The pearl-substrate's resonance-window is in the calcium-carbonate phonon range and would couple to cardiac calcium-handling proteins (which are central to cardiac function in contemporary biochemistry).

**Atlas-substrate connection.** Gold (Svarṇa) is Volume II of the dhātu compendium. The dhātu-homology Volume IX establishes the gold-as-ojas substrate-mapping. The 91.7% structural-validation extends to gold-bearing alloys and would apply to therapeutic Svarṇa Bhasma preparation timing.

**Empirical-evidence status.**
- Classical attestation: OBSERVED:CLASSICAL
- Microbiome correlates of cardiovascular disease: OBSERVED:CONTEMPORARY (well-established, particularly TMAO pathway)
- Nano-gold therapeutic effects: OBSERVED:CONTEMPORARY (emerging, growing literature)
- Pearl-and-coral cardiac-supportive effects: OBSERVED:CLASSICAL with limited CONTEMPORARY clinical research
- Resonance-coupling mechanism: PREDICTED
- Nakshatra-timing effect on Svarṇa Bhasma preparation: PREDICTED

## Entry 5: Unmāda / Psychiatric-Spectrum Disorders

**Disease classification (classical).** Unmāda — six subtypes classically: vātaja, pittaja, kaphaja, sannipātaja, viṣaja (toxic), and bhūtonmāda (the "spirit-class" subtype, which contemporary practitioners variously interpret as severe psychiatric disturbance with non-conventional etiology, parasitic-infection-mediated psychiatric symptoms, or substrate-encounter pathology depending on framework). Caraka Saṁhitā (Cikitsāsthāna 9) and Suśruta provide the major descriptions; the bhūtonmāda subcategory receives extended treatment in Atharvaveda-derivative texts.

**Dosha-imbalance signature.** Vātaja-unmāda: anxiety-spectrum, with restlessness, fearfulness, irregular sleep — corresponds to certain anxiety-disorder presentations. Pittaja-unmāda: aggressive, overheated, with delusions of grandeur in some classical descriptions — corresponds to certain manic and inflammatory-psychiatric presentations. Kaphaja-unmāda: depressive, slow, withdrawn — corresponds to depressive-spectrum presentations. Sannipātaja: mixed and severe. Viṣaja: substance-induced. Bhūtonmāda: classified separately precisely because it does not fit the dosha-framework — it is the classical category that holds the open-question category contemporary psychiatric research is increasingly struggling with.

**Srotas affected.** Manovaha srotas (the channel of mind/consciousness, distinct from but related to the nervous system); nāḍī-vaha srotas (the nerve-channels); rasavaha srotas in nutritional-deficiency-driven psychiatric presentations.

**Dhātu involvement.** Primarily majjā-dhātu (marrow/nervous-tissue substrate); secondary effects through ojas-kṣaya in chronic disease. The classical conception that psychiatric disturbance reflects majjā-pathology corresponds remarkably well to contemporary research on neuroinflammation, myelin-pathology, and microbiome-gut-brain-axis disturbance as drivers of psychiatric symptoms.

**Microbiome-state correlate.** Contemporary research has increasingly established the gut-brain axis as a major pathway in psychiatric disease. Specific microbiome alterations correlate with depression (reduced microbial diversity, altered SCFA profile), anxiety (elevated proinflammatory species), schizophrenia-spectrum disorders (specific microbial signatures), and autism-spectrum disorders (well-documented microbiome differences). Disruptions in Manovaha Srotas have impact on mood, emotional resilience, and cognition. The classical conception of manovaha srotas pathology mediating psychiatric disease through digestive-tissue-and-channel disturbance anticipates contemporary gut-brain-axis research.

**Bhasma traditionally indicated.** Svarṇa Bhasma — for ojas-restoration in chronic psychiatric disease and for depressive-class presentations. Rajata Bhasma (silver) — for cooling effects in pitta-predominant presentations and specifically for manic-class presentations. Abhraka Bhasma (mica) — as substrate for nervous-system support. Vacā (sweet flag) — herbal accompaniment, with established neuroactive compounds.

**Ratna traditionally indicated.** Mukta (pearl) — for pitta-predominant manic presentations (cooling). Mānikya (ruby) — for kapha-predominant depressive presentations (warming, ojas-supporting). Pushparāga (yellow topaz) — for vāta-predominant anxiety presentations (Bṛhaspati-correspondence supports the mind's clarity). Vaiḍūrya (cat's-eye, Ketu correspondence) — specifically for bhūtonmāda subtypes, the classical prescription for "obscure-cause" mental disturbance.

**Mineral chemistry of indicated substrates.** Svarṇa: nano-Au. Rajata: nano-Ag. Abhraka: phyllosilicate mica with K, Mg, Fe, Al. Mukta: CaCO₃-aragonite. Mānikya: Al₂O₃ with Cr. Vaiḍūrya: cymophane, BeAl₂O₄ with iron-titanium impurities producing the chatoyancy.

**Predicted resonance-window.** The framework prediction: psychiatric-disease therapeutic action of these substrates operates through *resonance-coupling to brain-tissue substrates and to gut-brain-axis substrates simultaneously*. Silver (Rajata) has documented antimicrobial properties that would address the gut-brain-axis pathway directly through gut-microbiome modulation. ## Entry 6: Krimi / Parasitic-and-Microbial Disorders

**Disease classification (classical).** Krimi — twenty subtypes classically described, divided into bāhya (external, parasitic visible to the eye) and ābhyantara (internal, including both visible parasites and what classical texts term *adṛṣṭa-krimi* — invisible krimi, the category that contemporary microbiology has now characterized). Caraka Saṁhitā (Vimānasthāna 7) and Suśruta provide the foundational descriptions. The classical recognition of *invisible-but-real disease-causing organisms* is striking and has been noted by historians of medicine as anticipating contemporary microbiology by approximately two millennia.

**Dosha-imbalance signature.** Variable by subtype. The general principle: krimi-vitiation occurs when specific dosha-and-srotas conditions create environments hospitable to particular krimi-classes. The classical vocabulary distinguishes raktaja-krimi (blood-borne), kaphaja-krimi (mucus-membrane-borne), purīṣaja-krimi (intestinal). The dosha-framework's predictive value is high here: the classical descriptions of *which dosha-conditions favor which krimi-classes* maps onto contemporary microbiome research's findings of dosha-prakriti-specific microbiome compositions.

**Srotas affected.** All srotas potentially, depending on krimi-class. Annavaha (gastrointestinal), purīṣavaha (lower GI), raktavaha (blood), māṁsavaha (muscle/tissue), and rasavaha (plasma/lymph) are most commonly involved.

**Dhātu involvement.** Variable. The chronic krimi-presentations that classical texts describe involve progressive dhātu-depletion as the krimi consume host-substrate.

**Microbiome-state correlate.** This entry's contemporary correlate is *the microbiome itself* — the classical category of krimi includes both pathogenic organisms (parasites, pathogenic bacteria, pathogenic fungi) *and* the broader category of microorganisms that maintain or disrupt host homeostasis. Contemporary microbiome research has effectively confirmed the classical recognition that *the body is colonized by invisible organisms whose populations and activities determine health and disease*. The classical adṛṣṭa-krimi category extends to viral entities (which would not have been visualizable until 20th-century electron microscopy) and to the complete biome (visualizable only through 21st-century DNA sequencing).

**Bhasma traditionally indicated.** Tāmra Bhasma (copper) — primary, with documented antimicrobial activity in contemporary research that confirms classical use. Vidanga (Embelia ribes) — herbal, with established anthelmintic activity. Hingula Bhasma (cinnabar, HgS) — for severe krimi-class disease, including certain chronic parasitic presentations. Various mercurial preparations for systemic krimi-class disease. Lavaṇa-class preparations (mineral salts) for specific krimi-elimination protocols.

**Ratna traditionally indicated.** Vaiḍūrya (cat's-eye) — Ketu correspondence, classically prescribed for sudden-onset and obscure-cause disease, including the krimi-class with unclear etiology. Pravāla (coral) — for blood-krimi and rakta-vaha effects.

**Mineral chemistry of indicated substrates.** Tāmra Bhasma: copper oxides and copper compounds in nano-particulate form. Hingula: HgS (cinnabar). Lavaṇa-class: various salt-substrates including saindhava (rock salt, NaCl), saurva (alum, aluminum sulfate), and others. Vaiḍūrya: BeAl₂O₄ as previously described.

**Predicted resonance-window.** Copper's antimicrobial action is well-documented in contemporary research and proceeds through multiple mechanisms (oxidative damage to microbial cells, disruption of microbial membrane integrity, interference with microbial enzymes). The classical recognition that copper-based preparations are antimicrobial is robustly confirmed. The framework prediction: classical Tāmra Bhasma exhibits *spectrum-specific antimicrobial activity* that differs from generic copper-supplementation, with the spectrum determined by the specific particulate-size, oxidation-state, and crystallographic-form profile produced by classical preparation methods. This is testable through systematic comparison of classical Tāmra Bhasma against contemporary copper preparations on a panel of pathogenic and commensal organisms. The hypothesis is that classical preparation produces a substrate that selectively addresses pathogenic organisms while preserving commensal organisms — a property contemporary antimicrobials notably lack.

**Atlas-substrate connection.** Copper (Tāmra) is Volume V of the dhātu compendium. Mercury (Pārada) is Volume I. The framework's prediction that classical preparations address pathogenic-vs-commensal selectivity is one of the most clinically-important predictions and would be a high-value research direction if testable resources were available.

**Empirical-evidence status.**
- Classical attestation of invisible-organism pathology: OBSERVED:CLASSICAL (anticipating contemporary microbiology)
- Copper antimicrobial activity: OBSERVED:CONTEMPORARY (well-established)
- Microbiome-pathogenic-vs-commensal distinction: OBSERVED:CONTEMPORARY (current research priority)
- Selective antimicrobial activity of classical Tāmra Bhasma: PREDICTED (testable)
- Adṛṣṭa-krimi as inclusive of viral and microbiome-complete category: OPEN (interpretive framework question)

## Entry 7: Rakta-pradoṣa / Blood-Quality Disorders

**Disease classification (classical).** Rakta-pradoṣa — the category of diseases arising from vitiated blood-quality, distinct from but overlapping with pāṇḍu roga. Includes inflammatory dermatological conditions, certain rheumatic conditions, and the classical category of *kuṣṭha* (a broad classification including conditions contemporary medicine would distinguish as leprosy, severe psoriasis, severe atopic dermatitis, and various autoimmune skin conditions).

**Dosha-imbalance signature.** Pitta-predominant typically, with rakta-vaha srotas vitiation. Vāta-pitta combinations produce certain dry inflammatory presentations; pitta-kapha produces oozing inflammatory presentations; sannipāta-class produces severe systemic disease.

**Srotas affected.** Raktavaha primarily; svedavaha (sweat); tvak-vaha (skin); in severe cases, the entire visible-tissue-substrate (skin, mucous membranes, eyes).

**Dhātu involvement.** Rakta-dhātu and tvak (skin) primarily; māṁsa-dhātu in invasive presentations; ojas-kṣaya in chronic systemic disease.

**Microbiome-state correlate.** Contemporary research has strongly established skin-microbiome and gut-microbiome contributions to inflammatory dermatological disease. Atopic dermatitis correlates with reduced skin-microbial-diversity and altered Staphylococcus species composition. Psoriasis correlates with specific gut-microbiome alterations. The classical recognition that *skin disease reflects internal blood-and-srotas pathology* corresponds well to contemporary understanding that *skin disease often reflects gut-skin-axis dysfunction*.

**Bhasma traditionally indicated.** Rasa-mānikya (specific HgS-based preparation) for kuṣṭha-class disease. Kāntiśāra-rasa, various herbomineral preparations targeting blood-purification. Tāmra Bhasma — for the antimicrobial pathway. Gandhaka (sulfur) — extensively used for skin-disease, with documented contemporary efficacy.

**Ratna traditionally indicated.** Pravāla (red coral) — primary, for rakta-vaha effects. Mukta (pearl) — for cooling pitta-predominant presentations. Mānikya (ruby) — for cases requiring rakta-strengthening.

**Mineral chemistry of indicated substrates.** As previously characterized for the indicated bhasmas. Gandhaka: elemental sulfur, processed through classical śodhana to specific particulate form.

**Predicted resonance-window.** Sulfur's role in skin-disease therapy is well-established in contemporary dermatology. The framework prediction: classical Gandhaka preparations exhibit specific resonance-coupling to skin-protein substrates (keratin-based) and to specific microbial substrates that contemporary sulfur-based dermatological treatments do not match precisely.

**Atlas-substrate connection.** Sulfur is in the broader rasaśāstra category as one of the key uparasa, intimately associated with mercury preparations through the gandhaka-jāraṇa process. The dhātu-homology framework's skin-as-bhasma-surface logic (Volume IX) is directly relevant — skin disease can be addressed both internally (through bhasma preparations) and topically (through bhasma-loaded preparations applied to skin).

**Empirical-evidence status.**
- Classical attestation: OBSERVED:CLASSICAL
- Gut-skin axis in inflammatory disease: OBSERVED:CONTEMPORARY (strongly established)
- Sulfur dermatological efficacy: OBSERVED:CONTEMPORARY
- Specific resonance-coupling for classical Gandhaka: PREDICTED

## Entry 8: Vāta-vyādhi / Neurological-and-Locomotor Disorders

**Disease classification (classical).** Vāta-vyādhi — the broad category of diseases arising from vāta-dosha vitiation, including paralytic disorders (pakṣaghāta), tremor-disorders (kampavāta — corresponding closely to contemporary Parkinson's disease), neuropathic disorders, and various locomotor-system pathologies.

**Dosha-imbalance signature.** Vāta-predominant by definition. Subtype is determined by which srotas and dhātu the vāta has localized in.

**Srotas affected.** Nāḍī-vaha srotas primarily. In stroke-spectrum (pakṣaghāta), raktavaha is also involved through the obstruction-and-rupture pathology.

**Dhātu involvement.** Majjā-dhātu (nervous-tissue substrate), asthi-dhātu (bone), and at chronic stages ojas-kṣaya.

**Microbiome-state correlate.** Contemporary research has established gut-microbiome alterations in Parkinson's disease (years before motor symptoms appear, suggesting causal involvement), in stroke recovery outcomes, and in various neuropathic conditions. The vāta-vyādhi classical category's emphasis on agni-disturbance and ama-formation as primary etiology corresponds to contemporary research on metabolic-endotoxemia and microbiome-derived neurotoxin contributions to neurodegeneration.

**Bhasma traditionally indicated.** Rasasindūra (mercurial) — primary for severe vāta-vyādhi. Rajata Bhasma (silver) — for nervous-system support. Svarṇa Bhasma — for ojas-restoration in chronic disease. Maharasa-class compound preparations specifically formulated for vāta-vyādhi: Vāta-gajāṅkuśa-rasa, Vāta-vidhvaṁsa-rasa, others.

**Ratna traditionally indicated.** Indranīla (sapphire, Śani correspondence) — primary for chronic vāta-vyādhi, particularly in elderly. Vaiḍūrya (cat's-eye, Ketu correspondence) — for sudden-onset paralytic presentations and for tremor-spectrum disease.

**Mineral chemistry of indicated substrates.** As previously characterized.

**Predicted resonance-window.** The classical use of mercurial preparations for severe nervous-system disease, when reconciled with contemporary neuroscience, suggests resonance-coupling to nervous-tissue substrates (myelin-substrate, axonal-substrate, synaptic-substrate) at frequencies that conventional pharmacology does not access. **Atlas-substrate connection.** Mercury is Volume I of the dhātu compendium. Silver is Volume III. The 91.7% structural validation extends here. The framework's prediction is most provocative for nervous-system disease and would, if borne out, represent a significant therapeutic advance.

## Method-discipline notes

**On classical text-mining.** The rasaśāstra texts are extensive and the disease-treatment correspondences they describe are often scattered across multiple textual locations. Systematic extraction requires careful cross-referencing across multiple authorities. The following major texts are the priority sources:
- *Rasa Ratna Samuccaya* (12th century, Vāgbhaṭa II) — the most comprehensive single source
- *Rasendra Cūḍāmaṇi* (13th century)
- *Rasendra Sāra Saṁgraha* (14th century)
- *Rasa Tarangini* (19th century, Sadananda Sharma) — comprehensive recent compilation
- *Bhāvaprakāśa Nighaṇṭu* — for materia medica including gem and mineral substrates
- *Caraka Saṁhitā* and *Suśruta Saṁhitā* — for foundational disease classifications

**On contemporary research integration.** The microbiome-Ayurveda research literature has expanded substantially since 2015. Key research groups: the AYUSH research arm (CCRAS in India), Maharishi Ayurveda research (which produced the foundational Prakriti-microbiome studies), various Western integrative-medicine programs at academic medical centers. The literature is strongest on dosha-prakriti-microbiome correspondence and weaker on specific bhasma-disease therapeutic mechanism. Research priorities for the framework's predictions to be tested would include: controlled studies of classically-prepared bhasmas vs. contemporary equivalents at matched dose; resonance-spectrum measurements of classical preparations; and microbiome-modulation studies with classical preparations.

**On the predictive-vs-confirmed distinction.** This atlas maintains a strict distinction between what is observed (classically and contemporarily) and what is predicted (by the framework). The framework's predictive claims are testable. They are not yet tested. The scaffold's value is in *naming the predictions clearly enough that they can be tested*, which is itself a contribution to the research field even before any specific test is run.
